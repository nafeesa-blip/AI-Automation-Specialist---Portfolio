State contractor license scraper with AI enrichment & weekly sales signals

Data Engineer | Web Scraping & AI Data Enrichment

An insurance and surety agency wanted to know, every week, which contractors had a new license, a bond or workers’ comp policy about to expire, or a status change. I built a scraper for the state licensing board’s public lookup site that tracks every license, saves each change, adds the company website and AI labels, and delivers ready-to-call lists.

The challenge

The agency sells contractor bonds and workers’ comp insurance. The best time to call a contractor is when they have just been licensed, or when their bond or policy is about to expire. All of this is public on the state licensing board’s lookup site, but only one license at a time, and the board’s downloadable list does not include bond or insurance dates. The sales team was checking licenses by hand and missing most of the timing.

The solution

I built a weekly scraper that keeps an up-to-date copy of every license. It starts from the board’s public licensee list, adds licenses found through searches and number-range gap filling, then reads each license detail page for status, classifications, bond and workers’ comp details. Every change is saved as an event. Active contractors are matched to their websites, and AI labels what they do and who they serve. SQL views turn all of this into sales signals that go to Google Sheets and HubSpot every Monday.

How it works

  1. Build the license listThe public licensee file is loaded, then searches by classification and ZIP code fill in anything missing from it.
  2. Find new licensesNew license numbers are issued roughly in order, so the scraper checks number ranges just above the highest known ones to find licenses issued this week.
  3. Fetch each detail pageAn async httpx crawler reads every license page with a polite rate limit, session handling, rotating IPs and automatic retries.
  4. Parse and validateselectolax pulls 14 fields from each page, and Pydantic checks types and dates before anything is saved.
  5. Save every changePostgreSQL keeps the current record plus a history of status, bond and insurance changes, each with the date it was first seen.
  6. Find the website and label it with AIA search API finds the likely website, confirmed by matching the business name and phone. The Claude API reads the site and labels services, residential or commercial work and company size.
  7. Deliver sales signalsSQL views build weekly segments (new licenses, bond or comp expiring within 60 days, suspended, comp exempt), exported to Google Sheets and synced to HubSpot lists.

Technical challenges and how they were solved

  • No bulk download with bond and insurance data: detail pages scraped for every license, at a steady, polite rate
  • Finding new licenses quickly: number-range gap filling, checked against search results
  • Hundreds of thousands of pages a week: async requests, a resumable queue and only re-checking what is due
  • Page layout changes: schema checks that stop the run and alert before bad data is saved
  • Wrong website matches: a match only counts when the business name and phone agree

Responsible use

  • Only public license data that the board publishes for consumers to look up
  • No login areas and no personal data beyond what is shown on the public license page
  • Requests spread across the night with rate limits and backoff

Results

  • About 287,000 licenses tracked and fully re-checked every week in under 10 hours
  • Around 1,100 new licenses found each week, most within days of being issued
  • Thousands of bond, insurance and status changes captured as dated events
  • Websites found for 71% of active contractors, with AI labels to target the right ones
  • The sales team gets ready-to-call lists in HubSpot every Monday instead of checking licenses by hand
